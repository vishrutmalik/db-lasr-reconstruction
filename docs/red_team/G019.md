## Findings

### RT-G019-1 — BLOCKING (self-leak / PIT stamp falsified): interval closures are stamped at interval OPEN

`raw_security_master.delisting_date`, `raw_universe_membership.valid_to` and
`raw_classifications.valid_to` are emitted **inside rows whose
`knowledge_time` is the interval's opening** (listing date / `valid_from`,
midnight UTC). The closure is future information — a hazard delisting drawn
periods later — folded into a payload stamped years earlier. By the
generator's own criterion (the LT-004 note: *"knowledge_time is FALSIFIED to
the decision date"* defines the planted leak), these stamps are falsified for
the closure columns; unlike LT-004, they are **not** flagged in the sidecar.

Evidence (baseline, seed 1729, 40×6y — the CT-suite world):

- 9/41 master rows carry `delisting_date` strictly after their
  `knowledge_time` date (e.g. `SYN0003`: knowledge 2005-01-31, delisting_date
  2008-07-31 — 3.5 years of look-ahead).
- 15/47 membership rows and 18/82 classification rows likewise.
- After `truncate_tables(world.tables, 2008-01-31 23:59Z)`, the *surviving*
  rows still reveal **4 future delistings, 6 future membership exits, 8 future
  classification ends.**

Why this is blocking for the evidence chain, not a pedantic schema point:

1. `truncation.py`'s stated guarantee — *"every surviving row was knowable at
   as_of"* — is false at field level, and **LT-019 (the framework's single
   universal mechanical PIT probe) deletes rows, not fields**, so it can never
   catch a consumer reading `delisting_date`/`valid_to`. A pipeline that
   drops names just before their (future) delisting would pass LT-019
   bit-identity and be certified leak-free while being survivorship-
   contaminated. The shipped `test_lt019_future_truncation.py` proves
   row-complement exactness only — confirmed by inspection.
2. The provider declares these families `supports_pit=true` +
   `FULL_VINTAGES`. FULL_VINTAGES semantics require the closure to be a
   **separate, later-stamped vintage row** (open row at listing: closure
   row/update at the delisting's own knowledge time). LT-016's own pass rule
   ("universe queries at any t re-run after appending later membership
   records are unchanged") presumes exactly that append-only shape; the
   generator instead rewrites the closure into the original row.
3. `fetch_universe_membership(universe, start, end)` returns rows whose
   `valid_to` lies beyond `end` — future exit dates on the contract surface.

Interval containment (`valid_from <= as_of < valid_to`) is a legitimate query
a canonical layer may run — but nothing in the emitted data distinguishes the
legitimate use from reading the closure itself, and no downstream guard exists
yet (G020 unmerged). The ground-truth provider for all leakage evidence must
not require politeness from its consumers.

Reproduction: `tests/leakage/red_team_g019_pit_content_test.py::test_surviving_rows_carry_no_post_asof_interval_closures` (strict xfail).

De-block paths (either): emit closure vintages (second row / superseding row
with the closure's knowledge_time) — the honest fix; or amend `truncation.py`
to additionally MASK closure fields dated beyond `as_of` and document the
interval-containment-only contract as a formal assumption with a canonical-
layer guard tracked for G020.

### RT-G019-2 — BLOCKING (weekly worlds; latent monthly): terminal_return is a forward-return disclosure 14 days early

Every corporate action is announced at `effective_date − 14d 21:30Z`
(`_emission.py::base()`), and delisting/merger rows carry `terminal_return` —
which `_build_returns` writes **verbatim as the security's entire
effective-period return** (companion keeper test proves realized close-to-close
return equals the announced field to 1e-12). Prices do **not** react at
announcement. Consequences, measured:

- **Weekly grid** (`ScenarioConfig("baseline", …, frequency="weekly")`, public
  surface, no internals touched): **22 of 22** terminal events are knowable at
  the *prior decision close* (announcement Friday t−2 21:30 < close Friday t−1
  21:00). An honest PIT consumer of the CLEAN dataset knows next period's
  return exactly (−40% delistings, +15% mergers). Truncating at the t−1 close
  keeps the announcement row. This is unflagged, exploitable future knowledge
  in a world the sidecar calls clean.
- **Monthly grid:** 0 of 12 terminal events knowable at the prior period
  close (the 14-day lead lands inside the month) — catalog monthly scenarios
  are safe **at the period-close decision grain only**; any intra-period
  consumer (e.g. a daily-rebalanced diagnostic) is exposed.
- Catalog weekly scenarios (LT-011/LT-012) contain no terminal events today,
  so the shipped battery does not trip this — but `generate()` accepts any
  catalog scenario at any frequency, and G037's charter is precisely such
  sweeps.

Reproduction: `red_team_g019_pit_content_test.py::test_terminal_returns_are_not_knowable_at_the_prior_decision_close`
(strict xfail) + passing companion `test_terminal_return_field_equals_the_realized_effective_period_return`.

De-block paths: keep the announcement lead strictly inside one decision period
of the scenario's grid (e.g. `min(14d, period_length − 1d)`); or make the
price path react at announcement; or move `terminal_return` knowledge to the
effective date. Alternatively, constrain configs (typed refusal for weekly +
terminal events) and record the monthly intra-period caveat in the sidecar.

### RT-G019-3 — BLOCKING (provider truthfulness): fetch_estimates silently drops all but the newest fiscal year

`fetch_estimates` deduplicates on `(ticker, exchange, metric,
forecast_period)` — **without `period_end`** (`synthetic_provider.py:641`).
Every FY1 row for every fiscal year in the window collides onto one key.
Measured (baseline 24×6y, full estimates history window): **2 rows returned
where 12 distinct `(forecast_period, period_end)` series exist — 10 fiscal
years of estimates silently discarded.** All other fetch keys in the module
include the period identity (`fiscal_period` for fundamentals); this one is
plainly a bug, and it violates provider_contract.md §3's no-silent-truncation
rule. A-cand-4 documents collapsing *revisions within* a series to the
window-end-knowable one — not collapsing *series across fiscal years*.

Reproduction: `red_team_g019_provider_surface_test.py::test_fetch_estimates_serves_every_fiscal_year_in_window` (strict xfail).
De-block: add `period_end` to the dedup key.

### RT-G019-4 — NON-BLOCKING (leak-prone contract semantics, honestly visible): vintage='latest' serves post-window knowledge

`fetch_fundamentals(vintage="latest")` with a window ending mid-restatement
chain serves the **restated** row even when its `knowledge_time` is months
after the window end, and drops the as-reported vintage that *was* knowable
(measured: window end 2007-06-14 → served value published 2007-09-12; the
knowable 0.9698 as-reported value is absent from the frame). This **matches**
the written contract (CT-11: "latest equals max-knowledge row") — it is the
vendor-`latest_filing` semantic, deliberately mirroring A-001 — but it is a
loaded gun for consumers who read "latest" as "latest knowable": the knowable
vintage is *unrecoverable* from the returned frame, so post-hoc as-of
filtering yields absence, not the earlier vintage. Note the in-module
asymmetry: `fetch_estimates` applies a window-end knowability cutoff;
`fetch_fundamentals` does not. Mitigations that exist today and must not
regress (pinned by a passing keeper test): the served row carries its future
`knowledge_time` and `version_type="restated"` visibly. G020's canonical
assembly must use `vintage="all"`; recommend a docstring warning and an
assumptions-register entry alongside A-cand-1.

Reproduction: `red_team_g019_provider_surface_test.py::test_latest_fundamentals_vintage_leak_is_at_least_visible` (passes; guards visibility).

### RT-G019-5 — BLOCKING for seed sweeps (lying sidecar entries): the error seeder is not collision-safe

80-seed scan of LT-021 (catalog size), diffing the `clean` ablation against
the corrupted tables and cross-checking the registry both directions:

| Defect | Count | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Registry entry with **no data anomaly** | 6/80 seeds | `STALE_PRICE` anchored on a ticker's final bar → "close frozen for 1 bars" → value frozen to itself; nothing changed |
| Registry `detail` misdescribes data | 1/80 | stale anchored on an already-negated close: propagated `-125.30` across 6 bars (data now has 7 negative closes; registry says one negation + one freeze) |
| "row duplicated verbatim" no longer true | 2/80 | a later corruption mutated one copy of the duplicated pair (e.g. closes `66.74` vs `59.26`) |
| Corruption absent from registry entirely | 0/80 | none found (with 6-bar stale-run semantics) |

The shipped battery seed (20260723) is unaffected (`test_lt021_seeded_errors.py`
passes), and the shipped test `test_stale_price_runs_exist` would itself fail
on seeds 10/15/17/54/55/78 — i.e. the defect is real, seed-dependent, and
already incompatible with the module's own assertions under sweeps. G021's
contracted "recall 1.0, report diffs cleanly against the sidecar" is
unsatisfiable on ~9% of seeds through no fault of the quality layer. Also
note the locator gap: stale entries locate only the anchor; the up-to-5
following corrupted bars are locatable only by parsing free-text `detail`.

Reproduction: `red_team_g019_registry_and_rng_test.py::test_every_stale_registry_entry_has_a_real_anomaly[10|15|17|54|55|78]` (strict xfail, all six seeds).
De-block: re-draw the stale anchor when the realized run length is 1 (and
ideally when the anchor is pre-corrupted); record realized run dates in the
registry; assert pairwise-verbatim after all corruption passes.

### RT-G019-6 — NON-BLOCKING (teeth integrity): survivorship ablation leaks the dead names through unfiltered tables

LT-009 `survivorship_biased` filters exactly the four declared tables, and the
diff is **exactly** dead-name removal (verified row-multiset diff: 58 master
rows, 4469 bar rows, 4469 metric rows, 58 membership rows removed; zero added,
zero extraneous — minimal within scope). But the world's other tables are not
overridden: `raw_corporate_actions` still contains all **58 delisting rows**
(with terminal returns and dates) and `raw_classifications` **116 rows** for
the dead names. Any consumer that overlays ablation tables onto the clean
bundle receives a "survivorship-biased vendor" that still discloses exactly
who delisted and when — a detector can key on the cross-table inconsistency
instead of the return-level bias the teeth check is supposed to exercise.
Recommend also filtering `raw_corporate_actions` and `raw_classifications` in
the ablation.

### RT-G019-7 — NON-BLOCKING (structural look-ahead via event existence): action eligibility conditions on full-path survival

`_build_action_schedule` selects split/symbol-change/merger names from
`eligible = lifespan > T/2 and term_reason is None` — computed **after** the
delisting stage. A split or symbol-change announcement is therefore a
probability-1 guarantee that the name never hazard-delists in-sample: event
existence encodes future survival. In baseline (splits + hazard delistings
coexist) an action-reading consumer gets a small but structurally guaranteed
delisting-avoidance edge not recorded in the sidecar. LT-009 is unaffected
(no scheduled actions there). Real-world splits also correlate with health,
but not with certainty; recommend sampling action names from currently-alive
names only, or noting the guarantee in the sidecar.

### RT-G019-8 — NON-BLOCKING (timestamp edge): publication 21:30 vs bar close 21:00 at the window end

`fetch_estimates(end=D)` uses an end-of-day cutoff (D 23:59:59), so revisions
stamped 21:30 on D — thirty minutes *after* D's bar close — are included. A
consumer equating "window end" with "decision at D's close" ingests post-close
data. `knowledge_time` is carried, so a disciplined consumer can re-filter;
flagged so G020's as-of joins use the bar-close cutoff, not the calendar day.

### RT-G019-9 — NON-BLOCKING (CT accommodation exploitability): the amended CT-07/CT-10 leave interval tables unpoliced

The G019 amendments to the contract suite are individually defensible
(announcement_time is the schema-native knowledge column; estimates'
`period_end` is a forecast horizon; CT-10 mandates the stamp CT-07 would
otherwise flag). But jointly, for a *hypothetically dishonest* provider:

- CT-10's U3 check compares knowledge to `event_date`/`period_end` only —
  membership/master/classification frames have neither, so **backfilled
  knowledge stamps pass CT-10**. Concretely: the LT-016 `current_membership`
  ablation (knowledge_time backfilled to period 0 — the planted leak) would
  sail through the amended CT-10 if served as an honest table.
- CT-07's structural-column exemption (`knowledge_time`, `announcement_time`,
  `valid_from`, `valid_to`) means fabricated values in those columns are not
  a coverage violation anywhere.

Recommend (G018 follow-up, not a G019 code change): a CT-16 for interval
tables — knowledge_time must be ≥ interval open, and interval *closures* must
be justified by a knowledge event (which is RT-G019-1's fix surfacing at the
contract layer).

### (3) Attacks on the fixes

- **Closure-vintage semantics (RT-1 fix):** probed `fetch_universe_membership`
  around a real exit (SYN0001, exit 2007-11-30): window ending the day before
  → open interval; ending on the exit day → still open (closure publishes
  21:30, after the 21:00 close — honest); ending a week later → closed
  interval; window starting after the exit → interval absent. At any as_of,
  "open because alive" and "open because the closure is unpublished" are the
  same epistemic state — the correct PIT answer; no marker distinguishes them
  and none should. Provider snapshot surfaces (`fetch_security_master`,
  `fetch_classifications`) collapse to PK-unique latest views (verified: no
  duplicate keys).
- **Residual (NON-BLOCKING, register-worthy):** the *world/bundle* tables for
  master/membership/classifications now fail a naive
  `validate_rows` duplicate-PK check (9/15/18 legit vintage collisions in the
  baseline world) — the same pre-existing convention as multi-vintage
  `raw_fundamentals` (also raises), now extended to three more tables.
  Consumers of `ScenarioBundle` datasets (G020/G021) must be vintage-aware
  (collapse or knowledge-filter before PK validation). Documented in
  `world.latest_vintage_view` and `_emission` docstrings; should land in the
  assumptions register alongside A-cand-1 when G020 consumes bundles.
- **Residual (NON-BLOCKING, cosmetic):** disclosure-order oddity on symbol
  changes — the old segment's master closure vintage (stamped at its last
  trading day, e.g. 2007-02-28 21:30) precedes the symbol-change action's
  announcement (2007-03-16 21:30), so the master reveals "segment ended"
  ~2.5 weeks before the action explains why, and the name has no open master
  segment until the new ticker lists. Event-level only (payload date ==
  stamp date; no realized values, no future dates); could mildly confuse a
  securities-spine builder. Not a leak.
- **Announcement-lead clamp (RT-2 fix):** `max(scheduled, prior-period
  publication)` floor verified across 912 actions on both grids: nothing is
  knowable at the prior decision close, nothing announces after its effective
  date, dividend amounts embed only already-realized closes. Terminal rows
  stamp at effective 21:30 — truncation at the effective close correctly
  excludes them (the delisting becomes knowable with the same half-hour
  publication lag as everything else).
- **Absolute FY labels (RT-3 fix):** collision-impossibility verified —
  every series has exactly one `period_end` per label, revisions strictly
  knowledge-increasing, no duplicate stamps; the FY-horizon structure
  (revisions arrive from the year before the fiscal end through the end year)
  preserves the FY1/FY2 economics without the relative-label key collision.
- **Collision-safe seeder (RT-5 fix):** disjointness by construction held
  under the 80-seed rescan (duplicated pairs stay verbatim — appended copies
  live beyond the drawable range and originals are locked); the stale
  predicate requires ≥2 changeable same-ticker followers, killing the
  freeze-to-self class; the 500-attempt exhaustion path is a typed refusal;
  determinism across seeds re-verified by hash equality.
- **RT-7 fix has teeth:** across 60 baseline seeds, 16 of 117 split names
  delist *after* their split — the probability-1 "actions imply in-sample
  immortality" guarantee is gone. The residual conditioning (alive with a
  ±1-period cushion at the drawn period; merger acquirers tautologically
  un-terminated) is documented in the code and not exploitable at the
  period-close decision grain (announcements land after the prior close).
